\subsection{Dispersion moderation improves BARCS in genome-scale simulation}
\label{sec:crispulator}

We used CRISPulator to evaluate a FACS design under known truth
\citep{nagy2017crispulator}.  At the primary multiplicity of infection (MOI)
of 0.20, three prespecified seeds each generated 10,000 genes, five guides per
gene, four independent replicates, and low, bulk, and high fractions.  All
methods received the same counts and design.

BARCS-ST and BARCS-MOD use the same gene summary; BARCS-MOD first moderates
the guide-specific dispersion estimates.  Moderation increased average
precision from 0.902 to 0.919 and F1 at gene FDR 0.10 from 0.811 to 0.847,
while realized FDP remained nearly unchanged (0.065 and 0.066).

The moderation gain persisted across the evaluated thresholds at MOI 0.20 and
in the secondary MOI-0.30 analysis (Figure~\ref{fig:crispulator}).  Fitting
the 50,000 guide-level regressions required 70.1--102.8 seconds per primary
seed on the analysis system. MAGeCK-MLE slightly exceeded BARCS-MOD in AUROC
and average precision while operating at a much lower realized FDP;
CRISPhieRmix operated at a higher FDP
(Table~\ref{tab:crispulator-comparators}).  These differences limit
comparisons of F1 at a common nominal threshold.  In the broader 400-gene
comparison, edgeR-QL, DESeq2, and limma--voom matched or exceeded BARCS
ranking.  After matching methods on realized FDP, the comparison did not
distinguish the methods above an FDP of 0.005.
