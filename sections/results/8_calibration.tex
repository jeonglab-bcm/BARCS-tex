\subsection{A correlation-aware gene statistic keeps gene-level error rates
nominal}
\label{sec:calibration}

A calibrated gene test requires more than calibrated guide tests.  Guides that
target the same gene share every library, so noise that they share makes
their null scores positively correlated, and a Stouffer statistic that divides
by $\sqrt{m_g}$ then overstates the evidence.  BARCS therefore estimates the
average within-gene correlation $r$ of guide Pearson residuals, subtracts the
average correlation between guides of different genes, and divides the
combined score by $\sqrt{m_g+m_g(m_g-1)r}$ instead (Methods).  Because the
fitted mean absorbs every design effect, including a true gene effect, a real
signal does not inflate $r$; only shared noise does.

To test this correction, we simulated all-null beta-binomial screens across
sample sizes, mean guide counts, and dispersions, with three or five guides
per gene, and added a focused arm in which guides of the same gene had a
latent correlation of 0.4.  The full grid and a separate continuous-dose
diagnostic are described in Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:supp-calibration}.
With independent guides, BARCS estimated $r$ near zero and the gene-level
$p<0.05$ rate stayed between 0.000 and 0.070.  With correlated guides, the
guide-level rates remained at or below 0.048, but the uncorrected gene-level
rates reached 0.108--0.262 (Table~\ref{tab:calibration-grid}).  The corrected
statistic brought them to 0.018--0.052, close to the nominal 0.05, without any
negative controls or post hoc scaling.

In the real screens analyzed here, the estimated within-gene correlation was
small: 0.008--0.026 across the four Cas13 cell lines, 0.011 in the IL2RA
screen, and 0.011 in the CRISPRn screen of \citet{evers2016crispr}.  The
independence assumption of the Stouffer statistic therefore held
approximately in these data, and the correction changed the Cas13 average
precision by less than 0.003.  The correction matters when guides share
artifacts, and BARCS reports the estimate so that users can check it.

\paragraph{Denominator sensitivity in an external audit.}
An audit of the benchmark deposited by \citet{dempster2025fdr,dempster2025fdrdata}
examined a separate source of error: changing library totals by restricting
the input to null genes before fitting.  Because H.-H.J.\ also developed
CB\textsuperscript{2}, this is an audit of an author's prior method.  For the
same Avana null genes, restoring full-library CB\textsuperscript{2}
probabilities reduced discoveries at FDR 0.10 from 152 to zero.  BARCS was not
fitted in that comparison.  The largest cell-line-specific null $p<0.05$ rate
remained 0.145, so nominal calibration requires further checks beyond the
corrected discovery count.  The complete audit, including BARCS results on the deposited
PSN1 and DeWeirdt tables, is reported in Supplementary
Section~\ref{sec:supp-fdr-audit}.
