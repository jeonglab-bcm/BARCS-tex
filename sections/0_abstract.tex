\begin{abstract}
Pooled CRISPR screens increasingly use longitudinal, donor-adjusted, and
factorial designs, whereas beta-binomial screen methods have largely focused
on pairwise comparisons.  BARCS extends the library-total-conditional
beta-binomial model to guide-level regression with an arbitrary design matrix,
enabling estimation of time, covariate, and interaction effects.  In four
replicate-complete Cas13 screens, adding the intermediate time point modestly
improved essential-gene recovery.  Applying the same non-targeting-control
scaling rule to BARCS, MAGeCK-MLE, edgeR-QL, DESeq2, and limma--voom produced
similar calibration diagnostics, although the alternatives ranked essential
genes more strongly.  In an ordered-bin IL2RA screen, donor-adjusted BARCS
recovered 22 validated regulators with 49 calls, compared with 17 regulators
and 72 calls for the matched MAGeCK-MLE fit.  Cross-fitted control scaling
reduced the guide-level null $p<0.05$ rate from 0.133 to 0.049.  Simulations
showed gains from dispersion moderation and control-based denominators, with
substantial variation in the denominator effect across seeds.  A null grid
isolated gene aggregation under within-gene guide dependence as a source of
error; aggregation-matched control scaling reduced but did not eliminate the
inflation.  An external audit also identified a denominator-dependent
CB\textsuperscript{2} false-discovery result, while leaving broader calibration
concerns unresolved.  BARCS enables multivariable analysis within the
beta-binomial CRISPR model.  Its inferential use depends on independent
biological libraries, likelihood-compatible counts, and calibration evaluated
at the level of the reported statistic; correlated guides and partitioned bins
require further modeling.
\end{abstract}
