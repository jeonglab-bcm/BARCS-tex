\begin{abstract}
Pooled CRISPR screens increasingly use time courses, ordered FACS fractions,
donor cohorts, and factorial treatments, but beta-binomial methods for screen
data have been limited to comparisons between two groups.  Analyzing these
designs requires a model that keeps the full design, separates sequencing depth
from biological replication, and reports gene-level error rates that hold when
guides targeting the same gene share noise.  To meet these needs, we developed
BARCS (Beta-binomial Analysis and Regression for CRISPR Screens), which fits
guide-level beta-binomial regression on an arbitrary design matrix, takes its
degrees of freedom from independent libraries rather than from reads,
moderates guide dispersions across the library, and combines guides into a
gene statistic that accounts for within-gene correlation.  In four
transcriptome-scale Cas13 time-course screens, BARCS ranked essential genes as
well as MAGeCK-MLE, edgeR-QL, DESeq2, and limma--voom (average precision 0.876
versus 0.874--0.877) and recovered as many essential genes at a gene-level FDR
of 0.10 as the best alternative (0.767 versus 0.692--0.767).  In a
donor-adjusted IL2RA screen with four ordered FACS bins, BARCS recovered 23 of
26 validated regulators with 61 calls, compared with 17 regulators and 72 calls
for MAGeCK-MLE.  In simulations, dispersion moderation and control-based
denominators increased power, and the correlation-aware gene statistic held
the gene-level type I error at 0.018--0.052 when guides shared noise, whereas
the uncorrected statistic reached 0.254.  BARCS is available as an R package
at \url{https://github.com/jeonglab-bcm/BARCS}.
\end{abstract}
