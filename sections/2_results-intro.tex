BARCS extends the CB\textsuperscript{2} sampling model, which conditions each
guide count on the corresponding full-library total to separate sequencing
variation from between-library heterogeneity \citep{jeong2019beta}.  BARCS
retains this sampling principle but replaces the two-group mean comparison
with a regression coefficient, so time, dose, donor, batch, and interaction
effects can be estimated jointly instead of being reduced to pairwise
contrasts.  We evaluate this extension in longitudinal Cas13 and
donor-adjusted IL2RA screens, use simulations to isolate the contributions of
dispersion moderation and denominator choice, and examine the effect of
preprocessing in an external false-discovery audit.  Endpoint, serial-harvest,
copy-number, and multi-condition follow-up analyses are reported in the
Supplement.  The extension broadens the questions a beta-binomial screen model
can address, but BARCS is not numerically equivalent to the legacy
CB\textsuperscript{2} statistic, which uses different dispersion, weighting,
and degrees-of-freedom calculations.
