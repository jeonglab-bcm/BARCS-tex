\subsection{BARCS fits multivariable designs directly from guide counts}
\label{sec:overview}

BARCS takes a guide-count matrix, the library totals, and a sample design
matrix.  For guide $g$ in library $i$, the model is
\begin{equation}
K_{gi}\mid N_i\sim
\operatorname{BetaBinomial}(N_i,\mu_{gi},\rho_g),
\qquad
\logit(\mu_{gi})=\mathbf{x}_i^\top\boldsymbol{\beta}_g,
\label{eq:barcs-model}
\end{equation}
where $N_i$ is the full mapped-guide total recorded before any filtering,
$\mu_{gi}$ is the expected guide proportion, and $\rho_g$ is the guide's
overdispersion.  The design vector $\mathbf{x}_i$ can contain continuous
predictors, group or donor indicators, and interactions, and a prespecified
coefficient or contrast tests the effect of interest within the joint fit.
BARCS keeps the sampling principle of CB\textsuperscript{2}
\citep{jeong2019beta} but has its own estimation and finite-sample testing
procedure.

The analysis proceeds in three steps.  First, BARCS fits each guide and tests
the coefficient of interest against a Student $t$ reference whose degrees of
freedom come from the independent libraries.  Second, because a screen leaves
each guide only a few residual degrees of freedom, BARCS moderates every
guide's dispersion toward a trend in abundance fitted across the library,
which adds prior degrees of freedom to the reference distribution.  Third,
BARCS combines the guides of each gene with a directional Stouffer statistic,
reports the median guide coefficient as the gene effect, and widens the null
variance of the statistic by the within-gene correlation estimated from guide
residuals.  We refer to the full procedure as BARCS; where the simulations
isolate the effect of moderation, BARCS-ST denotes the unmoderated guide fits
and BARCS-MOD the moderated ones, both followed by the same gene summary.
Negative-control scaling, when applied, changes significance but not
coefficient estimates, and its placement is stated for each benchmark.

Full-library totals estimate changes in library share.  BARCS can instead use
control-based totals, which express abundance relative to a prespecified
control class; we examine this choice in the interaction simulation.  This
deliberate change of reference is distinct from recomputing totals after
guide filtering, which changes the model without the analyst intending it.
