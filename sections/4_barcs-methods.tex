\subsection{Model and inference}

For the model in Equation~\eqref{eq:barcs-model}, coefficients are estimated by
feasible iteratively reweighted least squares.  At each fitted mean, the
guide-specific overdispersion $\rho_g$ solves a Pearson equation and is
truncated at zero when the counts are not overdispersed.  Coefficient and
dispersion updates alternate to convergence.  A prespecified coefficient or
contrast is divided by its model-based standard error and compared with a
Student $t$ distribution using residual sample degrees of freedom.  This
plug-in calculation does not fully propagate dispersion-estimation
uncertainty.  Tests are two-sided, and guide- or gene-level probabilities are
adjusted by Benjamini--Hochberg.

BARCS-ST uses unmoderated guide statistics.  BARCS-MOD applies an
empirical-Bayes variance adjustment based on a trend in log mean abundance,
changing standard errors and reference degrees of freedom while retaining the
coefficient estimates.  Real-data benchmarks use unmoderated BARCS.
Supplementary Sections~\ref{sec:estimation} and \ref{sec:inference} give the
estimating equations and moderation procedure.

\subsection{Gene-level aggregation and control calibration}

The primary gene summaries use directional Stouffer aggregation.  Each guide's
two-sided probability is converted to a normal deviate carrying the sign of
its coefficient.  The sum of these deviates is divided by the square root of
the number of valid guides and converted to a two-sided gene probability.
The gene effect is the median guide coefficient.  The exploratory DeWeirdt
audit uses Fisher aggregation, as specified in Supplementary
Section~\ref{sec:supp-fdr-audit}.

Control scaling divides a signed-normal statistic by the ratio of its control
95th absolute percentile to the two-sided standard-normal 0.05 cutoff.
Ratios below one are set to one, so scaling cannot increase significance at
that reporting level.  Coefficient estimates remain unchanged.  This rule
adjusts an empirical tail; it does not guarantee calibration throughout the
null distribution.

The calibration unit and evaluation split differ by benchmark.  Cas13 uses
non-targeting pseudo-genes matched to the target-gene guide-count distribution
and applies the same rule after each method's gene summary.  Its reported
proxy-null diagnostic uses unexpressed targeted lncRNAs.  IL2RA uses
\texttt{bb\_calibrate\_controls()} at guide level, with deterministic five-fold
cross-fitting for the reported control rate and all controls for the production
fit.  CRISPulator scales guide statistics before Stouffer aggregation.
simCRISPR splits each control class into normalization/calibration and evaluation
halves.  The all-null grid learns a post-aggregation scale from one half of
the null genes and evaluates the other half.  These distinctions prevent a
guide-level control rate from being interpreted as evidence of gene-level
calibration.

The Stouffer denominator assumes independent null guide scores.  Shared
artifacts or library effects can violate that assumption and inflate gene
significance, as shown in Table~\ref{tab:calibration-grid}.  Matching the number
of control guides per pseudo-gene also need not reproduce the dependence among
guides targeting a gene.  The reported gene probabilities and nominal FDR
thresholds therefore require empirical validation.  Permutations used for that
purpose must preserve experimental blocking and within-gene guide structure.

\subsection{Denominator choice and effect interpretation}

The default denominator is the full mapped-guide total, recorded before guide
filtering and retained throughout fitting.  A coefficient describes a change
in the log odds of library share; for small guide proportions, it approximates
a log abundance ratio per unit of the predictor.  Widespread depletion can
increase the library share of unchanged guides
(Figure~\ref{fig:simcrispr}), so relative abundance alone does not establish an
absolute change.

Control-based denominators deliberately replace that reference with a
prespecified control class.  The simCRISPR analysis rescales control totals to
the mean library size and rounds them, as detailed in the Supplement.  These
are normalization-derived effective totals, so the literal full-library
sampling interpretation requires separate assessment.  Effects are relative
to the chosen controls; an absolute-abundance interpretation additionally
depends on their invariance.  A control class's library share alone cannot
establish that invariance because the rest of the library may also change.
Non-targeting controls provide a reference without cutting, whereas safe-harbor
controls include the shared cutting response.  This explicit normalization
choice differs from unintentionally changing the denominator by subsetting
before fitting.

\subsection{Longitudinal Cas13 analysis}

For HAP1, HEK293FT, MDA-MB-231, and THP1, days 0, 7, and 14 were fitted
jointly with continuous time and a replicate indicator.  K562 was excluded
because one day-0 replicate was absent.  BARCS, MAGeCK-MLE, edgeR-QL, DESeq2,
and limma--voom received the identical rounded matrix and tested the same time
slope.  Non-targeting guides were grouped into pseudo-genes matching the
target-gene guide-count distribution, and the same post-aggregation control
scaling rule was applied to all five methods.  A BARCS endpoint fit using only
days 0 and 14 tested the value of the intermediate time point.  Known
essential genes were positives; targeted lncRNAs with TPM equal to zero in
both available expression assays were proxy nulls.  That definition is
imperfect in a direction that favours conservative methods, so the proxy-null
rates are diagnostics rather than validated type-I errors.  Because the
deposited values were already normalized and ComBat-corrected, this analysis
was treated as a processed-count sensitivity study.

\subsection{Ordered-bin IL2RA analysis}

The IL2RA screen comprised four ordered FACS bins from each of three donors.
BARCS fitted one ordered-bin score with donor indicators, and MAGeCK-MLE
received the same design.  Of 593 non-targeting guides, four folds estimated
the control scale and the omitted fold evaluated it; this was repeated across
five deterministic folds.  A seeded permutation of fold assignments checked
that identifier ordering did not determine the result.  Method discoveries
were compared with 26 regulators
validated by individual knockout and flow cytometry.  Waterbear and MAUDE
results were taken from their published analyses of the same experiment.

\subsection{Simulation benchmarks}

CRISPulator generated three 10,000-gene FACS screens with five guides per gene
and four independent replicates.  BARCS-ST and BARCS-MOD differed only by
guide-dispersion moderation.  simCRISPR generated three factorial
knockout-by-treatment screens with 2,000 sgRNAs and three replicates per arm.
Its known interaction effects allowed direct comparison of full-library,
non-targeting, and safe-harbor denominators.  Control guides used for
normalization and calibration were held out from scoring in simCRISPR.
The all-null beta-binomial grid varied library number, abundance, dispersion,
and guides per gene, with a focused correlated-guide arm.  Calibration
specifications are given in Supplementary
Section~\ref{sec:supp-calibration}.

\subsection{External and supplementary analyses}

The external false-discovery audit re-evaluated the deposited Avana
CB\textsuperscript{2} probabilities using full-library totals and ran BARCS on
the deposited PSN1 and DeWeirdt normalized tables.  Supplementary analyses
include the serial-harvest HT-29 screen, a four-coefficient GSE70038 screen
with method-specific pathway enrichment, and the Sanson A375 endpoint and
copy-number audit.  The Supplement provides detailed preprocessing, design, and scoring
definitions, together with the complete external audit.
