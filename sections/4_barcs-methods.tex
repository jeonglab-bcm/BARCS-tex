\subsection{Model and inference}

We estimate the coefficients in Equation~\eqref{eq:barcs-model} by feasible
iteratively reweighted least squares.  At each fitted mean, the guide-specific
overdispersion $\rho_g$ solves a Pearson estimating equation and is truncated
at zero when the counts are not overdispersed, and the coefficient and
dispersion updates alternate until convergence.  We divide a prespecified
coefficient or contrast by its model-based standard error and compare the
result with a Student $t$ distribution whose degrees of freedom are the number
of independent libraries minus the rank of the design.  Tests are two-sided,
and guide- or gene-level probabilities are adjusted by the Benjamini--Hochberg
procedure.

By default, BARCS then moderates the guide dispersions.  Each guide's Pearson
variance inflation is shrunk toward a lowess trend in log mean abundance, with
the prior degrees of freedom estimated from the whole library by the
scaled-$F$ moment method of \citet{smyth2004linear}.  This changes the
standard errors and adds the prior degrees of freedom to the reference
distribution while leaving the coefficient estimates unchanged.  Moderation
runs whenever at least 50 guides are usable and can be turned off with
\texttt{bb\_screen(moderate = FALSE)}.  All real-data results in the main text
use moderated BARCS (version 0.2.0); in the simulation ablations, BARCS-ST and
BARCS-MOD denote the unmoderated and moderated fits.  Supplementary
Sections~\ref{sec:estimation} and \ref{sec:inference} give the estimating
equations and the moderation procedure.

\subsection{Gene-level aggregation and control calibration}

For directional Stouffer aggregation, we convert each guide's two-sided
probability to a normal deviate carrying the sign of its coefficient and sum
the deviates over the $m_g$ valid guides of the gene.  Under independence the
null variance of this sum is $m_g$; if the null scores of a gene's guides have
average correlation $r$, it is $m_g+m_g(m_g-1)r$.  We divide the sum by the
square root of the latter and convert the result to a two-sided gene
probability.  BARCS estimates $r$ during the guide fits as the average
correlation of the guides' Pearson residual vectors within genes, with each
gene weighted equally, minus the average correlation of guide pairs from
different genes, truncated at zero.  Because residuals exclude every fitted
design effect, a true gene effect does not inflate the estimate.  The gene
effect is the median guide coefficient.  The exploratory DeWeirdt audit uses Fisher aggregation, as
specified in Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:supp-fdr-audit}.

Control scaling divides a signed-normal statistic by the ratio of its control
95th absolute percentile to the two-sided standard-normal 0.05 cutoff. Ratios
below one are set to one, so scaling cannot increase significance at that
reporting level.  Coefficient estimates remain unchanged.  This rule adjusts
an empirical tail; it does not guarantee calibration throughout the null
distribution.

The calibration unit and evaluation split differ by benchmark.  Cas13 uses
non-targeting pseudo-genes matched to the target-gene guide-count distribution
and applies the same rule after each method's gene summary.  Its reported
proxy-null diagnostic uses unexpressed targeted lncRNAs.  IL2RA uses
\texttt{bb\_calibrate\_controls()} at guide level, with deterministic
five-fold cross-fitting for the reported control rate and all controls for the
production fit.  CRISPulator scales guide statistics before Stouffer
aggregation. simCRISPR splits each control class into
normalization/calibration and evaluation halves.  The all-null grid learns a
post-aggregation scale from one half of the null genes and evaluates the other
half.  A guide-level control rate therefore applies to guide tests and cannot
establish gene-level calibration.

Matching the number of control guides per pseudo-gene does not reproduce the
dependence among guides that target one gene, because control guides are
grouped at random.  The correlation correction above addresses that
dependence directly, and Table~\ref{tab:calibration-grid} evaluates it.
Permutations used to validate gene-level error rates must likewise preserve
experimental blocking and within-gene guide structure.

\subsection{Denominator choice and effect interpretation}

The default denominator is the full mapped-guide total, recorded before guide
filtering and retained throughout fitting.  A coefficient describes a change
in the log odds of library share; for small guide proportions, it approximates
a log abundance ratio per unit of the predictor.  Widespread depletion can
increase the library share of unchanged guides (Figure~\ref{fig:simcrispr}),
so relative abundance alone does not establish an absolute change.

Control-based denominators use a prespecified control class as the reference.
The function \texttt{barcs\_control\_totals()} holds the control class at a
constant share of each library, rescales the totals to the mean library size,
and rounds them, as used in the simCRISPR analysis.  The resulting effective totals
come from normalization; their compatibility with the full-library sampling
model requires separate assessment.  Effects are relative to the chosen
controls.  Interpreting them as changes in absolute abundance also requires
those controls to be invariant.  A control class's library share alone cannot
establish that invariance because the rest of the library may also change.
Non-targeting controls provide a reference without cutting, whereas
safe-harbor controls include the shared cutting response.  This explicit
normalization choice differs from unintentionally changing the denominator by
subsetting before fitting.

\subsection{Longitudinal Cas13 analysis}

For HAP1, HEK293FT, MDA-MB-231, and THP1, days 0, 7, and 14 were fitted
jointly with continuous time and a replicate indicator.  K562 was excluded
because one day-0 replicate was absent.  BARCS, MAGeCK-MLE, edgeR-QL, DESeq2,
and limma--voom received the identical rounded matrix and tested the same time
slope; BARCS was moderated as described above.  Non-targeting guides were grouped into pseudo-genes matching the
target-gene guide-count distribution, and the same post-aggregation control
scaling rule was applied to all five methods.  A BARCS endpoint fit using only
days 0 and 14 tested the value of the intermediate time point.  Known
essential genes were positives; targeted lncRNAs with TPM equal to zero in
both available expression assays were proxy nulls.  This imperfect null
definition can favor conservative methods, so the proxy-null rates serve as
diagnostics and cannot be treated as validated type-I errors.  Because the
deposited values were already normalized and ComBat-corrected, this analysis
was treated as a processed-count sensitivity study.

\subsection{Ordered-bin IL2RA analysis}

The IL2RA screen comprised four ordered FACS bins from each of three donors.
BARCS fitted one ordered-bin score with donor indicators, and MAGeCK-MLE
received the same design.  Bin locations were the conditional means of a
standard normal variable within intervals of probability 0.2, 0.3, 0.3, and
0.2.  We assigned the 593 non-targeting guides to five
deterministic folds.  For each fold, the other four estimated the control
scale and the held-out fold evaluated it.  A seeded permutation of fold
assignments checked that identifier ordering did not determine the result.  We
compared discoveries with 26 regulators validated by individual knockout and
flow cytometry, using published Waterbear and MAUDE results for the same
experiment.

\subsection{Simulation benchmarks}

CRISPulator generated three 10,000-gene FACS screens with five guides per gene
and four independent replicates.  BARCS-ST and BARCS-MOD differed only by
guide-dispersion moderation.  simCRISPR generated three factorial
knockout-by-treatment screens with 2,000 sgRNAs and three replicates per arm.
The known interaction effects let us compare full-library, non-targeting, and
safe-harbor denominators.  Control guides used for normalization and
calibration were held out from scoring in simCRISPR. The all-null
beta-binomial grid varied library number, abundance, dispersion, and guides
per gene, with a focused correlated-guide arm in which a shared gene-by-library
latent normal effect induced a correlation of 0.4 among a gene's guides.  Calibration specifications are
given in Supplementary Section~\ref{sec:supp-calibration}.

\subsection{External and supplementary analyses}

The external false-discovery audit re-evaluated the deposited Avana
CB\textsuperscript{2} probabilities using full-library totals and ran BARCS on
the deposited PSN1 and DeWeirdt normalized tables.  Supplementary analyses
include the serial-harvest HT-29 screen, a four-coefficient GSE70038 screen
with method-specific pathway enrichment, and the Sanson A375 endpoint and
copy-number audit.  These supplementary analyses and the external audit were
computed with BARCS 0.1.0, without dispersion moderation.  The Supplement provides detailed preprocessing, design,
and scoring definitions, together with the complete external audit.
