BARCS extends the library-total-conditional beta-binomial CRISPR model to
multivariable regression.  Its practical contribution is a common coefficient
interface for time courses, covariate adjustment, and factorial interactions,
with explicit treatment of library totals and control diagnostics.  The
sampling model determines how sequencing depth and between-library variation
enter the fit; the design matrix determines which biological question the
coefficient addresses.

The biological examples support using that interface while showing different
tradeoffs.  In Cas13, adding the intermediate time point improved
essential-gene recovery modestly.  At the same time, all four alternative
methods ranked essential genes more strongly, and aggregation-matched control
scaling produced similar calibration diagnostics across methods.  The
normalized, ComBat-corrected input further limits conclusions about the
underlying count likelihoods.  In IL2RA, donor-adjusted BARCS recovered more
validated regulators with fewer calls than the matched MAGeCK-MLE fit.
Waterbear and MAUDE recovered more members of the validation panel, and the
panel's limited coverage prevents interpreting recovery per call as positive
predictive value.  The value demonstrated here is the ability to fit the
specified effects and inspect their operating characteristics.

The simulation ablations separate two choices within BARCS.  Moderation
improved genome-scale F1, although MAGeCK-MLE retained slightly stronger
ranking and a lower realized FDP.  Control-based denominators improved
interaction recovery in every simulated seed, with a much larger gain in the
one seed where the full-library fit collapsed.  These denominator choices
change the reference for abundance: full-library effects describe changes in
library share, whereas control-based effects describe changes relative to the
selected control class.  Non-targeting and safe-harbor controls also differ in
whether they absorb the shared cutting response.  Their suitability depends
on the biological effect being estimated and on the behavior of the controls.

Calibration is a separate requirement at each reporting level.  The IL2RA
cross-fitting analysis supports a guide-level operating point, while the null
grid shows that correlated guides can inflate Stouffer gene probabilities
even when guide tests appear calibrated.  Scaling after matched aggregation
reduced this inflation but left appreciable error in some cells.  The
beta-binomial fit also uses plug-in dispersion estimates; a residual-$t$
reference does not fully account for their uncertainty.  Nominal gene-level
FDR thresholds in these analyses therefore lack a general calibration
guarantee.  Dependence-aware gene aggregation and calibration using blocked
permutations or independent controls are priorities for further development.

The external audit reinforces the need to distinguish statistical inputs from
inference.  Restoring full-library totals corrected the reported Avana
CB\textsuperscript{2} discovery count, but the residual null inflation and poor
DeWeirdt interaction recovery leave the broader calibration concern intact.
Preserving totals through filtering prevents an unintended change in the
model; it does not resolve the effects of dependence, processed counts, or
comparison-specific confounding.

BARCS is most directly interpretable with likelihood-compatible counts and
independent biological libraries.  Repeated harvests from one culture and
FACS bins partitioned from one donor require repeated-measures or joint models
to represent their dependence.  Donor indicators adjust mean differences but
do not supply that covariance structure.  For these designs, the present fits
provide exploratory coefficients and control diagnostics.  Confirmatory
applications require biological replication and validation of calibration at
the guide or gene level at which discoveries are reported.
